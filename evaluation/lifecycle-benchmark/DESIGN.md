# Lifecycle amendment benchmark: preregistration scaffold

**Status:** executable metric design with invented counterexamples. No real
workflow was enrolled, no reviewers were recruited, and no empirical outcome is
reported. The numeric margins in the demo exercise the calculator and are not
recommended trial thresholds. This document is ready to fill for preregistration;
it is not a completed or registered protocol.

## Target and contrasts

The target population is explicitly bounded computational workflows with material
claims for which evaluators can establish affected and unaffected decisions after
one declared change. Fix a candidate, claim inventory, artifacts, and evaluator
fault oracle before assignment. The outcome is a decision about continued reliance
on the old claim/release at a fixed horizon; it is not whether the new claim is
true. A correct response to an affected old decision is reconsideration, even if
the revised science ultimately reaches the same conclusion.

Use the manuscript's conditions: B0, version-pinned repository plus instructions;
B1, B0 plus structured claim/provenance records; B2, B1 plus independent execution;
M, B2 plus prospective predicates, exact-version authorization and amendment rules.
Match relevant information access, training support, tools and permitted budgets.
Record the actual expense of preparing additional M records. B1/B2 must not be
artificially deprived of version and identity information available in their
underlying artifacts.

Primary contrast: M vs B2, stale acceptance after an announced change, constrained
by maintained acceptance of unaffected controls and bounded false invalidation.
Secondary contrasts: M vs B1/B2 for localization; M vs blanket reverification for
total recovery costs at matched missed-affected decisions. Authority/scope mismatch
challenges can use a separate preregistered stratum, with the same offered-denominator
principle; do not pool them with amendment cases after observing outcomes.

## Fields to freeze before enrolling real trials

| Item | Required preregistration entry |
|---|---|
| Population and exclusions | Public workflows, eligible claim types, independent oracle feasibility, domain/access limits, exclusion rules and expected count |
| Assignment | Randomization unit, allocation, seed custody, blocking factors, evaluator blinding and operator carryover prevention |
| Horizon | Exact elapsed-time origin, deadline and late-record policy; record time of offered work separately from start and completion |
| Materiality/ground truth | Fixed affected and unaffected inventories; adjudicators, unresolved-science procedure, amendments and exclusions logged before unblinding |
| Margins | Smallest useful stale reduction delta>0; absolute control-acceptance floor alpha>0; maximum control loss eta; false-invalidation ceiling beta; localization sensitivity floor if claimed |
| Sample size | Independent workflow/operator clusters, assumed intracluster dependence, detectable effect, power/error budget and sensitivity analysis |
| Inference | Cluster-aware estimator and intervals, randomization/permutation procedure, missingness rules, co-primary gate error control, multiplicity and stopping rules |
| Resource accounting | Setup, review, execution, repair and administration; elapsed wait vs active effort; overlapping compute/agent billing views and currency date |
| Integrity | Immutable registry and software hashes; independent oracle custody; assignment and timestamp evidence; audit of all offered attempts |

These fields cannot be inferred from test outputs. Do not choose margins after
looking at a trial or describe the demo's descriptive gate as statistical evidence.
No numerical sample-size recommendation is defensible without the selected
workflow/operator design and meaningful margins.

## Registry and final decision records

The scorer consumes two inputs. The evaluator freezes `offered` rows with `id`,
`truth` in {affected, unaffected}, and `cluster`. The evaluated process supplies
outcomes containing only `id` and `decision`. It cannot supply its own ground truth.
The cluster identifies the relevant assignment/inference unit; the calculator
reports the number but does not implement clustered inference.

Final states at the declared horizon are:

- `accept`: continue relying on the old exact claim/release as current.
- `invalidate`: mark the old decision affected/pending reconsideration. This is
  not rejection of the scientific proposition or a claim that the successor fails.
- `abstain`: no supported reliance or targeted invalidation decision.
- `refuse`: work explicitly declined.
- `timeout`: offered work exceeded the horizon without a final decision.
- `missing`: generated by the scorer when an offered ID has no outcome record.

Abstention, refusal, timeout and missing records all remain in denominators and
are reported separately in the confusion table. An affected-case abstention is
not counted as correct localization. A missing record is not zero resource use.
The metric code cannot authenticate timestamps, detect excluded offers, or prove
an oracle independent. The registered harness must enforce those boundaries.

Scientifically ambiguous cases require evaluator adjudication. The binary scorer
rejects `truth=unknown`; it does not silently drop or relabel those cases. Preserve
and publish a separate register of all ambiguous/excluded cases, their assigned
condition and dispositions. An exclusion amendment after assignment must be
reported and analyzed for bias rather than used to improve scores.

## Exact metrics

Let A and U be the fixed numbers of affected and unaffected offered decisions.
For each stratum g, let n(g,d) count final state d. Define:

- stale acceptance = n(A,accept)/A;
- affected invalidation sensitivity = n(A,invalidate)/A;
- false invalidation = n(U,invalidate)/U;
- control acceptance = n(U,accept)/U;
- unresolved fraction in g = [n(g,abstain)+n(g,refuse)+n(g,timeout)+n(g,missing)]/|g|.

Each stratum has an exact partition: acceptance + invalidation + unresolved = 1.
Undefined denominators are `null`, never zero error. Rates use `Fraction`, not
floating-point rounding. The source ID registry prevents omitted results from
shrinking denominators. Repeated claims within one workflow are not independent
sample sizes. The stratified proportions in toy fixtures do not estimate the
prevalence of defects in deployed automated science.

`compare` supplies a **descriptive stale-reliance gate** for paired toy cases:

1. Candidate stale acceptance improves over baseline by at least delta>0.
2. Candidate control acceptance is at least alpha>0.
3. Candidate control acceptance loses no more than eta from baseline.
4. Candidate false invalidation is at most beta.

All four must hold; both A and U must be nonzero. Therefore refusing/abstaining/
omitting every offer cannot pass: its control acceptance is zero, violating
alpha>0. Invalidating everything also fails that floor. This is a short algebraic
property, not evidence that any actual protocol works. A candidate that abstains
only on affected offers and accepts all unaffected controls can improve stale
reliance; that is **not** evidence of localization. A localization claim additionally
requires its preregistered sensitivity endpoint and false-invalidation constraint.

The code returns the complete rates and cells even when a gate fails. An acceptable
error metric does not erase poor coverage, unresolved work or high cost. Statistical
confirmation requires predeclared uncertainty bounds for the joint gates; four
favorable point estimates are insufficient. Claims about cost savings require the
cost comparison and matched correctness constraints, not this error-only gate.

## Executable counterexamples

From the paper repository root:

```sh
python3 -m unittest discover -s evaluation/lifecycle-benchmark -p 'test_*.py' -v
python3 evaluation/lifecycle-benchmark/metrics.py
```

The four invented offers contain two affected and two unaffected decisions. A
baseline accepts all four. With toy delta=1/2, alpha=1/2, beta=1/4 and eta=0:

| Invented behavior | Stale acceptance | Control acceptance | False invalidation | Descriptive gate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Accept everything (baseline) | 1 | 1 | 0 | No improvement |
| Refuse everything | 0 | 0 | 0 | Fails control gate |
| Abstain on everything | 0 | 0 | 0 | Fails control gate |
| Omit every result | 0 | 0 | 0 | Fails control gate; all offers still counted |
| Invalidate everything | 0 | 0 | 1 | Fails control and false-invalidation gates |
| Oracle labels affected; accepts controls | 0 | 1 | 0 | Passes toy gate |

The oracle uses evaluator truth directly. It is a metric sanity check and upper
bound, not an agent result. `synthetic-counterexamples.json` is regenerated output
from these invented records. Ten named tests validate the counting and gating
semantics, including absent strata, duplicate IDs, outcome-supplied truth,
undefined rates and relative control-loss guardrails.

## Planned reporting packet

Release the frozen offered registry, exclusions/adjudications, versioned artifacts,
randomization protocol, source hashes, raw outcome log, final horizon projections,
complete metric cells, cluster-aware intervals and all cost components. Protect
reviewer identities and restricted evidence according to an approved research
protocol. If outcomes do not distinguish M from B2, or M costs more without meeting
predeclared benefit margins, report that result and favor the simpler practice
for the tested setting. These fixtures do not preempt that possibility.
