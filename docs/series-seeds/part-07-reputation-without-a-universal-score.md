# Reputation and credit without a universal score

“Trustworthy reviewer” is not a scalar property. A person may be excellent at numerical reconstruction, inexperienced with a particular instrument, conflicted on one project, and uniquely qualified to interpret another.

A distributed system still needs to route work and recognize contributions. The mistake would be to compress identity, competence, diligence, agreement, prestige, and scientific correctness into one number. Such a score would invite Goodhart effects, reputation laundering, reciprocal review rings, prestige recursion, newcomer exclusion, and penalties for useful dissent. It would also conceal why a reviewer was selected.

MCRP instead points toward typed, event-derived histories. A review record can show the exact scope examined, method used, evidence coverage, limitations stated, challenges raised, corrections made, conflicts disclosed, and later adjudications. Competency recognition and authorization remain separate attestations. Contribution credit records what a person or agent produced; subsequent evidence about whether that product held up is another layer, not a retroactive erasure of credit.

A community can then publish a reproducible routing policy: for this review, seek one person with recognized experience in the method, one with domain expertise, no declared conflicts, and no common controlling institution. Another community may accept different evidence and thresholds. Both policies should expose their roots, exclusions, time windows, and treatment of missing data.

This does not solve reviewer selection. Histories can be incomplete, identity evidence can be wrong, and communities can be captured. The design aim is narrower: make the inference inspectable and contestable, preserve raw events, and prevent one ranking from becoming protocol truth.

Once trust views are computed from event histories, they can change as the history changes. The next post follows that update loop and asks what “living” can responsibly mean for a scientific network.
