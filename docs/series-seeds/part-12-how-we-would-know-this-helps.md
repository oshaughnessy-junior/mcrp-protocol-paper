# How we would know this helps

A richer protocol is not an improvement merely because it records more structure.

MCRP could increase review burden, expose sensitive relationships, privilege technically sophisticated communities, or replace legible editorial judgment with opaque policy machinery. Its distributed-review direction should therefore end not with a manifesto but with a preregistered evaluation contract.

The comparison must be credible. Candidate baselines include repository plus open review and existing publish–review–curate workflows. Test releases should contain known defects, ambiguous evidence, dependency changes, conflicts, and adversarial events. Competing systems should receive equivalent resources and reviewers. Outcomes should include false acceptance, defect localization, time and cost, reviewer agreement, uncovered-scope visibility, stale-decision detection, recovery after correction, newcomer participation, privacy harm, and resistance to coordinated manipulation.

Systems measures matter as well: event-ingestion rate, deduplication, dependency-impact traversal, subscription fan-out, policy-recomputation latency, checkpoint recovery, partition behavior, and convergence after delayed delivery. But scale without epistemic benefit is not success. The central question is whether exact, portable review records help people find defects, understand scope, preserve disagreement, and update reliance decisions at acceptable cost.

The stop conditions should be public. Narrow the program if crosswalks require reinvention of established standards, if users cannot understand policy views, if automated routing worsens exclusion or harassment, if privacy costs are unacceptable, or if the approach does not materially outperform simpler open-review records. A negative result would still identify which pieces are useful independently.

This sequence has described a target: immutable scientific records, portable scoped attestations, federated discovery, plural trust views, and an event-driven update loop under bounded human authority. The next responsible step is not to declare a new publishing system. It is to build small interoperable trials and let the measurements decide what survives.

## Maturity work

- Convert the evaluation agenda into a small preregistration table with hypotheses, baselines, measures, and stop thresholds.
- Separate usability, epistemic, governance, privacy, and systems outcomes.
- Make clear that MCRP has not yet demonstrated improvement on any of these outcomes.
